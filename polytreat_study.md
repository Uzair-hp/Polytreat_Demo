### How to picture the main industrial problems

- **Cooling system:** Think of water carrying heat away from equipment. The US Department of Energy describes cooling-tower concerns including corrosion, scale, fouling, and biological activity; actual needs vary by system and water quality. [DOE: Cooling tower management](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/best-management-practice-10-cooling-tower-management)
- **Boiler:** A boiler heats water to make steam. The DOE handbook explains that residual oxygen can contribute to boiler corrosion and that water-treatment choices depend on operating conditions. This is why the catalogue has products it labels oxygen scavengers, scale controllers, and sludge conditioners. [DOE: Steam systems handbook](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2014/05/f15/steamhandbook.pdf)
- **Effluent treatment:** Tiny suspended particles can remain in wastewater. EPA materials explain that coagulation/flocculation helps bring fine particles together so they can settle or be separated. That is the broad idea behind catalogue terms such as “coagulating agent” and “flocculating agent”; product performance must come from its own technical sheet. [EPA: Coagulation and flocculation](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=20007RNK.TXT)
- **RO:** Reverse osmosis uses a fine, semi-permeable membrane to separate water from many dissolved substances. The USGS glossary explains that product water passes through the membrane while salts are retained in the reject stream. [USGS: Water Science Glossary](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-science-glossary)

These outside references explain general engineering ideas only. They do **not** validate any PolyTreat product or claim.

### First, what business area is this?

This catalogue is about products used to **keep water out of buildings, protect surfaces, help repair concrete, protect floors and metal, and seal joints**. These are different jobs, so one product should not be described as a universal waterproofing solution.

An easy way to picture it: some products are like a flexible raincoat painted onto a roof; some are additives that help repair mortar stick; some are tougher protective layers for industrial floors or steel; and some fill cracks or joints. These are analogies to help learn the categories, not product performance claims.
